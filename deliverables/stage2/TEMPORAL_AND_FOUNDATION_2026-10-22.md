# Temporal and foundation models for CS2 win probability and tracking data

Date: 2026-10-22
Built from: the three family surveys supplied for this run (family 1: CS temporal and graph models; family 2: foundation and pretrained tracking models; family 3: spatio-temporal GNN options). No paper outside those surveys is included. Nothing was downloaded or run.

## How to read this

- **Verified: yes** is recorded for every row because each entry was opened at its own page, PDF, TeX source, repo or model card. Where a number came from a summarizing fetch tool, the cell says "(summary)"; from an abstract page only, "(abstract)"; from a search snippet, "(snippet)". Where the surveys disagree, the row says so and section 6 lists it.
- **Temporal** means the model uses more than one time step.
- **Code and weights "none"** means the survey found no link. It does not prove that no code exists.
- Per-step cost is reported for only a few entries (section 3). Most are "not reported".
- Numbers across papers are not comparable: different games (CS:GO vs CS2), maps, years, splits and metrics.

What Table 1 says:
- The bar is logistic regression. Huang et al. report logistic at in-time AUC 0.8519 and out-of-time AUC 0.8450. Their TCN, Transformer and GAT sit within a few thousandths of it, inside the CIs. Nothing here shows a temporal model clearly beating a calibrated logistic regression on a fair held-out split.
- Szmida and Toka report a temporal GNN AUC of 0.8549 against a logistic AUC of 0.7564, a gap of about 0.10. Huang et al. find about zero gap for their temporal models. The two papers differ in data, features and split, and the surveys do not explain the difference, so neither gap is a like-for-like architecture effect.
- The user's "temporal GNN already built for CS" is most likely Szmida and Toka, the only temporal GNN for CS in the surveys. This is an inference, not confirmed with the user.

Cost per step, what is reported:
- MLMove: under 0.5 ms per game step, amortized, on one CPU core (authors' claim). The surveys do not define a game step, so this cannot be turned into a per-second cost.
- TPNet: up to 33.3x faster than its SOTA baseline (summary). No absolute time.
- SNAM: 9x to 100x faster epochs (claim, under review).
- HAWKEYE: 5 minutes per pass on tgbl-flight (67M edges; summary). A per-pass number on a large dataset, not per step.
- All other entries: not reported.

Our estimate, from architecture sizes and not measured: with 10 player nodes, hidden size 64 and 16 input features, one 2-layer GAT pass costs roughly 6e4 multiply-adds, and one GRU step with hidden 64 costs roughly 2.5e4. With cached history, one new second costs roughly 1e5 multiply-adds. Re-encoding a full 20-second window costs roughly 2e6. Compute is therefore not the constraint on CPU at 1 Hz; framework overhead and data loading will dominate. Training cost scales with data: 476,595 in-time snapshots (Huang et al.) and about 90,000 windowed graphs (Szmida and Toka).

### Use now (code and license allow it, with the caveats shown)

2. **CS-NET v4 code** (MIT). Read the path model and the single-tick heads. ### What we would have to build

1. A demo-to-per-second table: for each second, the 10 players (position, velocity, view, HP, armour, equipment, kit, side, alive), bomb state and timer, round clock, alive counts and team economy. 3. Baselines: calibrated logistic regression and LightGBM on the same features, reporting log-loss, Brier, ECE and AUC with match-level bootstrap CIs.

## 5. Recommendation

**First model to build:** a causal per-second GAT over the 10 player nodes, feeding a causal TCN (or GRU) over the last 20 to 40 seconds, plus a small global state vector (bomb status, round clock, alive counts, economy). Output is P(CT wins) for each second. Train from scratch on our CS2 demos, with a calibrated logistic regression as the bar.

Why this one:

1. It is the smallest model that combines the two pieces the surveys report separately: a graph over players (Huang et al.'s GAT, Xenopoulos and Silva's GAT, Szmida and Toka's spatial GNN) and causal time (Huang et al.'s causal TCN, Szmida and Toka's GraphGRU).
2. It needs no released weights and no restricted code. Huang et al.'s repo has no license, so we rebuild from the paper's description, which we have to do anyway for our own data.
3. The surveys set the bar. Calibrated logistic regression reaches in-time AUC 0.8519 and out-of-time AUC 0.8450 on 220 matches. A temporal model has shown nothing until it beats that on out-of-time log-loss and on contested states. The plan is built to test exactly that.
4. Graph features helped only together with state features (Xenopoulos and Silva 2022: GAT alone loses to the state baseline; GAT plus state is best). So the first model includes state features, not positions alone. The ablation that matters is positions-only, state-only and both.
5. Compute is small (section 3 estimate), so CPU serving at 1 Hz is realistic.

What not to build first:

- **CS-NET v4 as the first model.** Its win-rate heads are single-tick, it has no metric, its training data is undocumented, its step is 0.25 s, and its checkpoints total about 1.4 GB and need your approval. It is a candidate for initialization or frozen features once the baseline exists.
- **Foundation pretraining** (UniTraj, TranSPORTmer, the masked autoencoder, JointDiff). None has CS2 data, and there is no CS2 checkpoint to start from.
- **Diffusion generators** (JointDiff, U2Diff, heteroscedastic diffusion). They generate trajectories and do not classify round outcomes, and they need several denoising passes per step.

What to expect: the surveys do not show that any temporal or graph model beats a calibrated logistic regression on a fair held-out split. The first build is a test, not a promised gain. Szmida and Toka's 0.8549 is on an unknown split, and Huang et al.'s 0.8531 ensemble sits inside the logistic CI, so neither is evidence of a gain over our own baseline. The project's own v3 win-probability model is not compared here; the surveys do not cover it.
