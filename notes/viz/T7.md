## Readout

The two maps with the most clean 5v5 rounds are de_mirage (6,870 rounds, 344 matches, 49,188 enemy kills) and de_dust2 (6,248 rounds, 315 matches, 44,642 kills). Kills per round are almost identical: 7.16 [7.13, 7.19] on Mirage and 7.15 [7.11, 7.18] on Dust2, and the intervals overlap. Both maps are strongly concentrated. The top 10% of the 40 x 40 cells by count hold 62.4% of kills on Mirage and 57.6% on Dust2. The hottest cell on Mirage holds 544 kills at world (-1214, -695), and 501 of those victims are CT. The hottest cell on Dust2 holds 595 kills at world (607, 715), and 484 of those victims are T. Half of the Dust2 hot-cell kills (50.8%) fall in the first 15 seconds after freeze end, against 37.9% on Mirage, which suggests an early-round contest on Dust2. That reading is an inference: no radar image is available to confirm what the locations are. The chart is in raw CS2 world coordinates, and the panel says so.

## Method

- Map name: `header.map_name`, joined on `header.key` = `round_end.match_key`. All 32,041 clean rounds mapped.
- Kill: a `player_death` row in a clean round where `attacker_id` is present, differs from `player_id`, and both team codes are present and differ. Position: `player_x_pos`, `player_y_pos` (victim).
- Grid: 40 x 40 square bins. Both panels use the same window side (4,346.9 world units, the larger of the two maps' own windows), each centred on that map's kills. Colour is the kill count per cell on a linear scale, using a single-hue Blues ramp, with one maximum (595) shared by both panels.
- Intervals: match-cluster bootstrap. Matches are resampled with replacement, 1,000 resamples, `random.Random(0)` (a fresh generator per map). The 95% intervals are 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles with linear interpolation. Two statistics: kills per round, and the share of kills in the top 10% of cells (cell set fixed from the point estimate).

## Caveats and assumptions

1. No radar image. Panels are in CS2 world coordinates with y up. The y-up convention is an assumption and was not checked against a map image.
2. The victim-position field was checked against the 1 Hz sample. For 19,415 death/sample pairs with the sample taken within one second before death, the median distance from `player_x_pos`/`player_y_pos` to the victim's own sampled position is 19.5 units. The attacker's position is a median 657 units away. This check was an ad hoc run and is not in `T7.py`.
3. Grid sensitivity. An earlier run sized each panel's window to its own kills (Mirage window 4,160.3 units), which gave Mirage a top-10% share of 59.3% and a hottest cell of 617. The shared window used in the chart gives 62.4% and 544. Treat cell-level and top-decile figures as descriptive and binning-dependent.
4. Colour scale. Most cells sit at the low end of a linear scale. The median non-empty cell holds 39 kills on Mirage (731 non-empty cells) and 37 on Dust2 (798 non-empty cells), against maxima of 544 and 595. The colour is dominated by a few hot cells.
5. Intervals cover only kills per round and the top-decile share. There are no per-cell intervals. They do not include binning uncertainty or map-pool uncertainty. The top-decile cut (the 160th cell by count) is 106 on Mirage with 4 cells tied at that count, and 91 on Dust2 with 2 cells tied. Ties are broken by cell index order.
6. Population includes overtime. Clean rounds run up to round 48 across the tome. Overtime effects on density were not checked.
7. Team codes: T = 2 and CT = 3, as given in the brief. Mirage victims: 25,963 T, 23,225 CT. Dust2 victims: 23,056 T, 21,586 CT. Team-kill and unknown-team rows are excluded from the kill count.
8. Not checked: whether the hot cells correspond to named positions, and whether the chart renders correctly in a browser. The SVG was checked structurally (element count, numeric coordinate ranges, no NaN, and an estimated text-box overlap check that found none). The 1 Hz sample was used only for the validation in caveat 2.
