BRIEFING: WIN PROBABILITY IN CS2 (27 OCTOBER 2026)
Plain text. Times are Central.
Numbers are log loss unless marked. Lower is better. A 50/50 guess scores 0.693.
Validation = the 1,168 matches used to choose between options.
Test = the Mirage test, 687 matches, read many times since 23 Oct.
Fresh = 653 matches from 22 to 25 Oct, built and checked, never scored.


PART 1. WHAT HAPPENED, IN ORDER

1. 14 Oct. Integrity rules pinned: splits by match, bootstrap intervals, exclusions.
   The tree model was kept, because research found no sequence or set network
   beating a tuned tree model. Foundation models tested: worse by 0.009 to 0.013.

2. 22 Oct. Stage-two plan (an assistant proposal; no owner approval pinned):
   a set encoder, and a temporal model over the last 20 to 40 seconds.

3. 23 Oct. First read of the Mirage test. 2024 February kept separate.
   Matches from 17 to 21 Oct added to training.

4. 24 to 25 Oct. Many checks on the Mirage test: the all-maps sequence model,
   distances, walking routes, defuse timing, word2vec, map control.
   Walking did not beat straight-line on test.
   A matched-scope check found the sequence model better than the tree model
   on test, by 0.00166 (pin 2e4130fe).

5. 26 Oct. Validation-only pre-registrations start.
   The straight-line distance gain was re-checked on validation with site centres
   fitted on training matches only. It held (pin ee61ba3a).
   Tests run: plain network, attention, defuse speed, event counts, sanity check.
   The 50/50 blend was pinned with a read-once rule.

6. 27 Oct. The 22 to 25 Oct matches were merged into the tomes. The first merge check
   failed; the store was restored; the amended check passed.
   Manifest checked. Learning curve run. Six correction pins written.
   The blend's one-time read was re-pinned to the 653 fresh matches (pin dd78df27).
   Deck and fact sheet built.

PART 3. WHAT THE RESULTS ADD UP TO (our interpretation; check it before saying it)

- Gains that held on validation with rules fixed in advance:
  the distance features (R2, and the contested-round gain in R7),
  and the sequence model (R4), which grows with more data (R6).
- Things that did not clear the bar: the learned structure on top (R9 to R13, R15, R16)
  and the graph variant (R17).
- The blend (R5) is the best measured result. It is measured on validation only.
- Working idea: the model improves when it knows where the bomb could go off, and it
  improves with more training data. Learned structure on top added nothing at this data size.
- What would change this: the blend failing to beat the distance model on the
  653 fresh matches.


PART 4. COMPLICATIONS

C1. The Mirage test (687 matches) has been read many times since 23 Oct.
    88 unpinned result files touch test-split numbers. 87 report a test outcome.

C2. Several choices were first checked on the test set: distance features,
    walking versus straight-line, defuse timing, word2vec, and the THGNN graph.
    Only the straight-line distance has a validation check (R2).
    Walking versus straight-line, defuse timing and the THGNN graph have none yet.

C3. The site centres were fitted with test matches included. The straight-line
    gain was re-checked with training-only centres and held. The walking arm and the
    sequence model's distance arm were not rebuilt with training-only centres.

C4. The blend was chosen after the test numbers for its two parts were already on record.

C5. The training split for the all-maps runs is inferred, not recorded. Their row counts
    match the 21,468-entry manifest exactly, but the saved JSON has no manifest hash.
    The shipped v6 model file was trained on a 12,563-train manifest.

C6. Reproduction: a CPU retrain of the tree model (arm A, seed 0) missed the 1e-4
    prediction gate. Log loss matched to 2e-5, but single predictions differed by up to
    0.126. The GPU that would settle it is busy with another project's job.

C7. Validation covers all maps (1,168 matches). The clean test numbers are Mirage only.
    Claims about other maps rest on validation alone.

C8. The fresh 653 matches have 11 duplicate copies outside every split.
    How to handle them is undecided.

C9. The 22 to 25 Oct matches are in the tomes but not yet in the model tables
    (the snapshot and the geo join). No run uses them.

C10. The learning curve is one seed, with the sequence model's epoch picked on validation.
     It used 19,478 matches where the pre-registration said 21,468 entries.

C11. The blend is not servable yet. In production, the sequence model needs the last
     20 seconds of per-second state. That path has not been checked.

C12. Licence: the navigation mesh derives from Valve game data. Internal research use only,
     until a licence review (download approved; pin c432a055).

C13. Sources. Huang et al. 2026 (the source of the 250 units per second and of the
     contested-round rule) is not found online. The Chahrour 2023 paper is not found;
     we used its repository and a manuscript page. The Szmida and Toka year is open
     (2025 by paper ID, 2026 by listing). Our walking formula has not been compared
     with Xenopoulos 2020, Section III.

C14. The 20 seconds is a count, not a duration. The paper's window is 20 snapshots at
     4 Hz, which is 5 seconds. Ours is 20 seconds at 1 Hz.

C15. The reading list has 28 numbered entries, not 27: 4 tested, 2 related, 22 not tested.

Slide 9. Why a sequence model, and why 20 seconds.
  Say: 14 Oct we kept the tree model. 22 Oct the plan proposed a sequence model over
  20 to 40 seconds. 25 Oct the earlier tree-model edge came from scope; on matched scope the

  every seed (validation). The learning curve (R6): more matches help the sequence model most.
  Watch out: the 25 Oct comparison was on test. The 20 seconds is a count; the paper's
  window is 5 seconds (20 snapshots at 4 Hz). Our recurrent part is a GRU, the same family as
  the THGNN recurrent layer, and the CS:GO GRU precedent on our reading list
  (Leathart and Polaczuk 2020) also uses one.
