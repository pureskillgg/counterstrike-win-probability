## Takeaway

No paper in the 2023-2026 window reports a held-out, round-level log-loss comparison of a graph or player-interaction model against a tabular baseline for CS2, Dota 2, LoL, or Valorant. The one direct head-to-head is Xenopoulos and Silva (2022, CS:GO, 14.3M per-second states from 7,222 matches, 70/10/20 split). Their state-vector baseline reached log loss 0.4351, the best model (GAT with state features) reached 0.4276, and a GCN alone reached 0.6111. That is a small gain (about 0.0075 log loss), it came from one split with no significance test, and the graph helped only when the global state vector was included. The 2026 CS2 (Szmida and Toka) and LoL (Airoso da Silva and Lopes) papers report accuracy or AUC with no baseline in the text I could read, so they cannot be compared with our test log loss of 0.4366. Our data is smaller (about 230k event-derived states from about 1,500 matches), and Xenopoulos's rows are per-second snapshots, so counts are not like-for-like. My inference, not a sourced claim: a graph over 10 player nodes is small enough to train on the RTX 3080 and run on CPU, but the evidence says the gain over a well-featured state model is modest. The better-supported next step is to add per-player features (alive, equipment, position and bombsite distance) to our existing feature set through a small attention or set-pooling model, and to score it with log loss on the same held-out split as XGBoost before committing to a full GNN.
