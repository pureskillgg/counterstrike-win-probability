## Takeaway

Round-level evidence for Valorant, Siege, and Overwatch is thin. The only round-level models with numbers are a VALORANT economy-plus-ultimate logistic regression (60.61% on 1,301 rounds; SNIPPET only, not reproduced) and a TimeSformer video model that reaches 80.55% per-second accuracy overall but only 56.32% in the first 24 seconds and 89-91% in the second half of the round (OPENED; its 100-round evaluation set doubled as the early-stopping validation set). The vlr.gg streak table (OPENED) shows round win rates from 37% to 62% by prior-five-round wins, but it is one team and unadjusted for economy. The only Siege first-kill figure (67%, 45 of 67 rounds) is a single event with a wide interval, and the Siege and Overwatch accuracy figures come from pre-match or draft features, with the 93.75% Siege result probably inflated by fold leakage. For our model this suggests three things. A round-start economy-only baseline in VALORANT lands near 60%, so economy alone is modest signal. The one neural result here is a video model whose gains come from in-round intelligence events we do not have, so it does not justify a neural network on our event-derived states. And at test sizes near 150 matches, as in the NCI thesis (XGBoost 73%, CatBoost 72%, hybrid 70%), differences of one to three points sit inside sampling error (about +/-3.6 points at one standard error), so our model comparisons need intervals before we conclude anything. The design to copy is Hayakawa's accuracy-by-round-time table: report our log loss by round-time bucket and by bomb state, not only overall.
