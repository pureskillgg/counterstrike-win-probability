## tabular-fm-core
**Angle a summary (agent):** Verdict: a small in-context tabular foundation model (TFM) is worth a bounded test against our tuned XGBoost, but I found no 2025-2026 paper that settles it for our case. The evidence is mostly accuracy, rank or Elo on TabArena/TALENT, mostly on small-to-medium tables. The million-row claims are self-reported by the model authors. None of the sources I read reports log loss or calibration (ECE) against tuned XGBoost on a multi-million-row, match-grouped task. The non-IID question (dependence within a match) is the one none of these sources answers.  Recent papers (2026, newest first in the list below): TabICLv2 (Feb), TabPFN-3 (May), LimiX-2M (Jun), TabDPT-Turbo (Aug), Localized TabICLv2 (Aug), LimiX-2 (Sep), TabPFN-3.5 (Sep). Only the first six are in the sources list; TabPFN-3.5 and LimiX-2 are unverified leads (see below).  Key findings: - Large-table scale: TabICLv2 reports 1M samples x 500 features in 450 s with disk offloading (under 24 GB CPU, 50 GB GPU). That is batch throughput, not per-query real time. TabPFN-3 claims 1M rows on one H100 via a reduced KV cache and row-chunking. - Against tuned GBDT: TabPFN-3's abstract says it beats 8-hour-tuned gradient-boosted trees on tables up to 1M rows and 200 features. TabICLv2's text gives average ranks on the TALENT subset with more than 10K samples: Stage 3 4.71, RealTabPFN-2.5 6.35, and Stage 1 14.91, described as comparable to XGBoost at 14.60. The rank table does not state XGBoost tuning. - Log loss: TabICLv2 names log loss as the multiclass metric on TabArena and says it matches RealTabPFN-2.5 in log loss after about 200K pretraining steps. That is a training curve, not a final benchmark score. No ECE or calibration numbers were found in any source read. - Splits: TabArena uses repeated cross-validation and TALENT uses 64/16/20. No source I read states a group or match-level split. - Real-time serving: in-context models condition each prediction on training rows, so cost grows with context size. Localized TabICLv2 keeps 98.64% of full TabICLv2 accuracy with about 249x median single-query serving speedup (TabArena classification, accuracy only). No source reports absolute CPU latency. Our demand is one prediction per match-second, so the budget is roughly up to 1 s per query per live match. That needs measurement on our hardware. - Lightweight: LimiX-2M has 2M parameters and its abstract claims it beats larger TabPFN-v2 and TabICL on widely used benchmarks. The abstract gives no numbers, and I did not verify its code licence. - Fine-tuning: Localized TabICLv2 reports Stage 2 and Stage 3 fine-tuning. Whether fine-tuning on real tables improves log loss is not confirmed. - Licences and deployment: licence status is unclear for several models (TabICL weights not stated; TabPFN-3 licence unconfirmed; LimiX states Apache 2.0 for released models). TabPFN and TabICL are PyTorch-based, and the GPU environment does not have PyTorch yet, so serving would add a dependency. **Angle b summary (agent):** Bottom line: none of the six sources shows an independent result where a pretrained tabular foundation model (TFM) beats tuned XGBoost on large, grouped or correlated data under binary log loss. The strongest 2026 claim (TabPFN-3, vendor-run) says it beats 8-hour-tuned GBDTs at 100k to 1M rows, but only on 13 datasets and with relative scores. The strongest independent large-data test (Belgian and French motor insurance, about 678k policies) finds a GLM and XGBoost ahead of TabPFN on claim frequency; TabPFN's frequency deviance stayed in a narrow band (about 0.34 to 0.38 on freMTPL2) across in-context sizes from 2,000 to the full training fold and never reached the GLM's 0.296.  By dataset size: pretrained in-context learners look strongest on small to mid-size data (TabArena, already on your list; TabPrep's gains peak at 10k to 100k rows). TabICL reports it beats TabPFNv2 and CatBoost on 53 TALENT datasets above 10K rows, but the text I read gives no per-dataset numbers. Trees and MLPs dominate under temporal splits (TabReD) and under feature or distribution shift (open-environments study).  Log loss and calibration: none of the six reports binary log loss on large data. The metrics are AUC, accuracy, Elo, RMSE and Poisson or gamma deviance. We would need to fill this gap ourselves.  Grouped or correlated rows: I found no independent evaluation of TabPFN-style models on grouped or clustered rows. The nearest evidence is TabReD, where random splits overstate performance and time splits reorder methods. BeyondArena (arXiv 2606.30410, already on your skip list; not re-cited) includes grouped and temporal task types and reports that trees and deep models still dominate on non-IID, large and high-dimensional data. Its abstract matches the pattern found here.  Leads not verified at source: a commercial blog quotes TabArena v0.1.4 Elo of 1673 for TabPFN-3 against 1375 for tuned XGBoost; I could not confirm these in the TabPFN-3 text I read. Serving constraint: the insurance paper reports TabPFN inference time grows steeply with context size, reaching several minutes at capacity for v2.6 and much lower for v3. **Angle b summary (agent):** Angle b (fine-tuning tabular foundation models) has usable 2025-2026 evidence, but no source tests fine-tuning on match-clustered data, and none gives calibrated CPU latency for a fine-tuned model.  What the evidence says: 1. Full fine-tuning is the default recommendation for TabPFN v2 (Rubachev et al. 2025). It beat zero-shot on most i.i.d. tasks and beat tuned XGBoost on several (not Adult). It was less stable on the temporally drifting TabReD subsets, where it hurt on Sberbank Housing. LoRA did not beat full fine-tuning and was slower in their timing table. 2. Across tabular foundation models, full supervised fine-tuning often reduced accuracy and calibration (Tanna et al. 2026). TabPFN's ECE stayed near 0.03 under full fine-tuning, while TabICL, TabDPT and OrionBiX ECE rose about 2-3x. Meta-learning and LoRA gave only situational gains. Most models overfit below about 1,000 rows. 3. Context selection: LoCalPFN (Thomas et al. 2024) builds a per-query local context from nearest neighbours (k = min(10*sqrt(n), 1000)) and fine-tunes on it. Its AUC was 0.958 vs 0.943 for tuned XGBoost (IQM, 95 datasets). It is slower than trees per query, and no CPU latency is reported. Correlated rows are not studied. 4. Post-hoc calibration: the MAKE 2026 benchmark (36 OpenML-CC18 datasets) found calibrator choice mattered more than tree-vs-ensemble. Temperature scaling was best for neural models and Dirichlet for trees, and the authors recommend choosing calibrator and architecture jointly. Its TabPFN v2 sub-results were not readable (the full text returned HTTP 403). 5. Grouped data: the TabPFN-3.5 technical report (Sept 2026) notes that baselines with group-aware validation can exploit group structure while full-training-set models cannot. This is an evaluation-design warning, not a fine-tuning result.  Implications for our project (inference from the sources, not stated by them): - The Prior Labs default fine-tuning validation is a 10% hold-out that the docs do not describe as grouped. For match-clustered data, pass match-grouped X_val/y_val or early stopping will look optimistic. - Any fine-tuned model needs its own post-hoc calibration on held-out matches, compared with XGBoost's current calibration. - The 1,000-row warning is a row-count heuristic. With about 3,700 matches, the effective sample size is much smaller than 4.5M rows. - Keep a fine-tuned model only if it beats both default TabPFN and tuned XGBoost on match-held-out log loss.
